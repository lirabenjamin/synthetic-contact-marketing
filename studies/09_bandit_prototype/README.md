# 09 Click-bandit prototype of the SunCell game

Standalone, playable prototype of the redesigned allocation game: a two-armed bandit framed as money in, money out (BenBen, 2026-11-07). The player has a $100,000 budget and invests it $1,000 at a time. Each click invests in Riverton (R-leaning) or Lakewood (D-leaning) and shows the sales money that came back, with a green number rising from the button (always green). Each county's average return is fixed for the whole game: $1.35 back per $1 in Riverton, $1.00 in Lakewood. One return is Gamma-distributed with the same mean and noise as the Poisson sales in `simulate.py`. Bonus: 2.5¢ per $1,000 of profit, never below $0 and capped at $1.00 (BenBen: luck counts, bonus is the game money scaled down, up to a dollar). The tutorial has 3 screens and a 2-question check; there is no belief question, no weeks and no diminishing returns, and county cards are neutral gray.

Open `prototype.html` in a browser. URL parameters: `r`, `d` (average $ back per $1, defaults 1.35 / 1.00), `kr`, `kd` (noise shape, defaults 2.7 / 2.0; SD of one return = mean / sqrt(shape)), `budget`, `unit`, `cents` (bonus per $1,000 profit), `seed`, `dev=1` (researcher panel with the truth). The end screen offers the investment log as JSON. `screenshots/` holds static renders (`?shot=tut1|tut2|tut3|game0|game|end`).

## Design simulation (`simulate.py`, version a)

`python3 simulate.py` (about 80 s) writes `output/power.csv`, `output/curves.csv`, `output/fig_curves.png` and `output/fig_power.png`. Simulated Democrats are greedy Bayesian learners (Gamma-Poisson) whose prior about Riverton starts at the pilot's control mean (log ratio -0.35, SD 0.4); the chat shifts that prior by +0.1 or +0.2 (d = 0.25 or 0.5). Grid: Riverton sells 1.2x / 1.35x / 1.5x per visit, prior worth about 5 or 20 visits, 100 to 300 people per condition. These simulated people embody assumptions, not pilot estimates of the click game.
